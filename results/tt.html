Double-transverse proton–proton collisions

The NLO hard part of the double-spin asymmetry $A_{TT}$ of single-inclusive hadron production in collisions of two transversely polarized protons, $a^\uparrow b^\uparrow\to c+X$. known, rederived

What is computed

Each incoming quark or antiquark carries the chiral-odd density $\tfrac12\not k_a\gamma_5\not s_a$ (and likewise for $b$), whose partonic cross section multiplies its transversity distribution $\delta f$; the observed parton is unpolarized. The cross section depends on the azimuth $\Phi$ of the observed parton about the common spin axis as $\cos 2\Phi$, and the hard part is the coefficient of $\cos 2\Phi$ in $\tfrac12[\sigma(\uparrow\uparrow)-\sigma(\uparrow\downarrow)]$, written in the distribution form below.

For a measured parton of momentum $p$ the partonic cross section $\sigma = s\,E\,d\sigma/d^3p$ is written in $v = 1 + t/s$ and $w = -u/(s+t)$, with $s=(k_a+k_b)^2$, $t=(k_a-p)^2$, $u=(k_b-p)^2$ for incoming partons of momenta $k_a$, $k_b$ and $0 < v,w < 1$:

$$\sigma = \sigma_{\rm Born}\,\delta(1-w) + P(v,w)\,c_{\rm reg}(v,w) + P(v,1)\big[c_{0}(v)\,\big[\tfrac{1}{1-w}\big]_+ + c_{1}(v)\,\big[\tfrac{\ln(1-w)}{1-w}\big]_+ + c_{\delta}(v)\,\delta(1-w)\big],$$

Here $\sigma_{\rm Born}$ is the lowest-order term, and $c_{\rm reg}$, $c_0$, $c_1$, $c_\delta$ are the coefficients $R$, $B$, $C$, $A$ of the paper, with $P$ its normalization $\mathcal N$. The scales enter through $L_R=\ln(\mu_R^2/s)$, $L_F=\ln(\mu_F^2/s)$ for the incoming partons and $L_{FF}=\ln(\mu_{FF}^2/s)$ for the fragmenting one ($\mu_{FF}$ is the $\mu_F'$ of the paper).

Here $P = \alpha_s^3/(\pi s v w)$; $L_F$ refers to both transversity densities. Only five of the sixteen channel classes contribute: a gluon has no transversity, and the two helicity flips must sit on one fermion line of the cut diagram (annihilation, or the exchange interference of identical quarks). Class 7 starts at order $\alpha_s^3$.

The calculation

The diagrams, the squaring, the reduction and the counterterm formulas are those of the unpolarized and longitudinal classes. The spin vectors are written in the basis of the scattering plane, $s=\cos\varphi\,e_X+\sin\varphi\,e_N$, with $e_X$ in the plane along the transverse momentum of the observed parton and $e_N$ the normal, and the squares are computed as the components $XX$ and $NN$ (the mixed ones vanish by parity, which is checked). The normal component of the unobserved momentum is reduced through the Gram matrix of the three external momenta, the normal component of the loop momentum by symmetric integration. In the HVBM scheme the symmetric part $(XX+NN)/2$ is evanescent but not zero in $D$ dimensions; the hard part is $(XX-NN)/2$, and the symmetric part is carried along as a check: its finite remainder vanishes in every class, so no finite renormalization is needed. Counterterms: the transversity kernel $P^T_{qq}(z)=C_F[2z/(1-z)_+ + \tfrac32\delta(1-z)]$ ($\delta P_{qq}$ in the code) on both incoming legs, the unpolarized kernels for the fragmenting parton, with the $D$-dimensional TT Born table.

The reference is the Fortran code of Mukherjee, Stratmann and Vogelsang (2005), transcribed verbatim into Mathematica; its slots hold one half of the $\cos 2\Phi$ coefficient, a factor fixed on the Born term and confirmed by the renormalization- and factorization-scale terms. The four coefficients are evaluated with 30-digit arithmetic at six rational kinematic points and $N_f=4,5$ (12 SU(3) points), using distinct fixed renormalization, initial-state and fragmentation scales. The required SU(3) scaled residual is below $10^{-20}$. Additional $C_A=2,5$ diagnostics, with $C_F=(C_A^2-1)/(2C_A)$, are reported separately; the reference code's general-color validity is not assumed. The Born terms equal the lowest-order cross sections of Soffer, Stratmann and Vogelsang (2002). Inside the notebooks: poles cancel, the scale derivatives are those of the evolution equations, the real-emission squares are gauge invariant, and the squares of the three routes agree (exact arithmetic at two random rational points).

All five classes are reduced and assembled separately in each of the three algebra routes. Their squared matrix elements agree at two exact-rational points, and their finite coefficients are compared at six sampled points.
