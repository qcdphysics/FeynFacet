INDEPENDENT TRANSVERSE-TRANSFER FINITE ASSEMBLY

Scope. The new program uses the existing D-dimensional Born tensors and scalar
Reduced.wl real/virtual integrals as mathematical inputs. It does not load the
FeynFacet package or step 4. Transfer.wl is loaded only as the comparison target.
This is an independent assembly/subtraction implementation, not an independent
amplitude calculation or master-integral evaluation.

Normalization. For a Born square Q=|M|^2/g_s^4, define
 B_D(s,v)=alpha_s^2 Q(s,-s(1-v),D)/(s v), D=4-2epsilon.
This follows directly from flux 1/(2s) and the tagged-particle measure
d^3p/[(2pi)^3 2E]: sigma=sE d sigma/d^3p gives |M|^2 dPhi_X/(32pi^3).
At Born level dPhi_1=2pi delta_+(s4), with delta(s4)=delta(1-w)/(s v),
giving |M|^2/(16pi^2 s v). Inserting g_s^4=(4pi alpha_s)^2 yields B_D.
All O(epsilon) terms in Q and its incoming-gluon averages are retained. In the
common coefficient convention, real emission carries
 mu_R^(2epsilon) (4 pi alpha_s)^3/(32 pi^3) times Reduced.Real/g_s^6.
Virtual interference carries 2/(4 pi)^(2-epsilon) times the Born phase-space
normalization (4 pi alpha_s)^3/(16 pi^2 s v), from converting the loop measure
d^D l/(2pi)^D to d^D l/(i pi^(D/2)) and taking 2Re(M1 M0*).
The actual scalar-input definition was checked in
Processes/HadronicTransfer/2-Algebra-FeynCalc.wl: the virtual constructor
contains I average[incoming] normalize[...], with no factor two and no loop
measure factor. It therefore supplies i c_j M0* times the scalar masters.
The phase and factor two in the independent assembler follow this definition;
neither is fitted to real-emission pole cancellation.

Collinear convolution derivation. Write every LO kernel as
 P(z)=r(z)+c[1/(1-z)]_+ +d delta(1-z).
Resolving the lower Born delta yields W(w,epsilon)P(z(w)), where z(1)=1.
The maps follow by solving the lower-process Born condition s'+t'+u'=0,
with w'=-u'/(s'+t') and v'=1+t'/s'. For incoming a,
(s',t',u')=(zs,zt,u), hence z=w and v'=v. The convolution measure dz/z,
Born scaling B_D(zs,v)=B_D(s,v)/z, and delta(1-w/z) give B_D(s,v)/w.
For incoming b, (s',t',u')=(zs,t,zu), hence z=(1-v)/(1-vw), v'=vw.
The absolute inverse derivative of 1-w'(z) at the zero is z^2 vw/(1-v).
Together with dz/z and Born scaling 1/z this gives B_D(s,vw)vw/(1-v).
For outgoing c, (s',t',u')=(s,t/z,u/z), hence z=1-v+vw and v'=vw/z.
The inverse Born-delta derivative is vw and the fragmentation measure is
dz z^(2-D), giving the density below.

The resulting three maps and densities are

 incoming a: z=w; W=B_D(s,v)/w;
 incoming b: z=(1-v)/(1-vw); W=B_D(s,vw) vw/(1-v);
 outgoing c: z=1-v+vw; W=vw/z^2 B_D(s,vw/z) z^(2epsilon).

For the outgoing leg, the D-dimensional fragmentation factor is z^(2-D),
namely z^(-2+2epsilon). After the z^-2 factor is included in W, the residual
factor is z^(+2epsilon). Its derivative gives +2 log(z) B_0, not -2 log(z).

Define A(w,epsilon)=W(w,epsilon)(1-w)/(1-z(w)), A1=A(1,epsilon).
Using an analytic regulator eta in the original splitting variable,
 (1-z)^(-1+eta)=delta(1-z)/eta+[1/(1-z)]_+ +O(eta),
and 1-z(w)=z'(1)(1-w)+O((1-w)^2), the pushforward is

 ordinary: r(z(w))W(w)+c[A(w)-A1]/(1-w),
 D0:       c A1,
 D1:       0,
 delta:    [d+c log z'(1)]A1.

This single identity implements all three legs; there are no separate copied
route-specific tuples. The endpoint slopes are 1, v/(1-v), v respectively.
The endpoint logarithms follow from the kernel's original plus prescription
over 0<z<1, including the part below the physical z lower bound.

Transversity kernel: CF[-2+2/(1-z)_++3/2 delta(1-z)], with no gluon or flavour
mixing. The unpolarized second incoming leg uses the usual qq,gq,qg,gg kernels.
Quark flavour sums use Nf-1 only when the other flavour is not externally fixed.
Charge-conjugate channels have the same Born tensor and are explicitly mapped.

Multiply each collinear distribution by
 alpha_s/(2pi epsilon) exp[epsilon(log4pi-gamma_E+log(mu_R^2/mu_leg^2))]
and extract its epsilon^-1 and epsilon^0 coefficients, so evanescent Born terms
enter the finite remainder. Coupling renormalization replaces the distribution
by -beta0 B_D delta, beta0=(11CA-2Nf)/3, with scale mu_leg=mu_R.

Real-emission endpoint derivation. Introduce h=1-w. Each noninteger power
base(h)^q is factored by its endpoint valuation m into h^(m q)a(h)^q,
a(h)=base(h)/h^m with a(0) nonzero. The regulator slope in h is therefore
known algebraically without changing the original numerator dimension D.
For each branch h^(-1-lambda epsilon)B(h,epsilon), subtract the endpoint
of a smooth test function before expanding in epsilon. The endpoint integral
is int_0^1 dh h^(-1-lambda epsilon)=-1/(lambda epsilon).
Writing B=b[-1]/epsilon+b[0]+epsilon b[1], the distribution coefficients are

 epsilon^-2 delta: -b[-1](0)/lambda;
 epsilon^-1: ordinary [b[-1](h)-b[-1](0)]/h,
             D0 b[-1](0), delta -b[0](0)/lambda;
 finite: ordinary {b[0](h)-b[0](0)
                    -lambda[b[-1](h)-b[-1](0)]log h}/h,
         D0 b[0](0), D1 -lambda b[-1](0), delta -b[1](0)/lambda.

The program obtains the branches using endpoint valuations, expands them with
this test-function subtraction, and inserts the shared master functions only
after the distribution coefficients are built. It compares real terms,
virtual normalization, every counterterm component, both pole coefficients,
and the final regular/D0/D1/delta coefficients against the stored results.
It also compares the independently assembled XX and NN finite coefficients
after applying CF=(CA^2-1)/(2CA). Generic logarithm identities use factorwise
real-sign proofs; an uncertified factor or nonpositive total argument leaves
the original logarithm intact. No unconditional power/log expansion is used.

Sensitivity tests perturb each finite basis coefficient in both components of
every class. In class 1 they also rebuild the actual counterterms with the
evanescent Born terms omitted, or the fragmentation epsilon factor omitted,
and require the same acceptance predicate to reject the altered result.
Separate small analytic controls exercise the corresponding generic terms. An additional
direct integral in the original splitting variable checks the pullback for
all three kinematic maps against polynomial test functions. These are
validation tests, not numerical cross-section predictions.
The elementary Integrate calls check splitting-kernel distribution identities
against test functions. They do not integrate real-emission phase space or
replace the reverse-unitarity/IBP/master-integral calculation supplying Reduced.
