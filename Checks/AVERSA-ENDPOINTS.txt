ANALYTIC q g -> q ENDPOINT BENCHMARK

Reference
F. Aversa, P. Chiappetta, M. Greco and J.-P. Guillet,
QCD Corrections to Parton-Parton Scattering Processes,
Nucl. Phys. B327 (1989) 105-143,
https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(89)90288-5.

The benchmark transcribes the printed formulas: distribution convention in
Eq. (22), quark finite scheme functions in Eq. (23), Born term in Eq. (41),
and the E01 (q g -> q + X) endpoint coefficients in Eq. (43). Relevant
printed pages are 113-114 and 127-128. It uses CQ=0 in Eq. (43); the finite
quark functions f_qq and d_qq are exceptions to the paper's CQ switch and
therefore remain present at CQ=0 (see the paragraph after Eq. (23)).
No Fortran output or process-specific assembly helper is used.

What is compared
The inputs are the UU Born term and three endpoint coefficients of class13
in Coefficients/ProtonProton.wl. Write B(v)=s*Born/alpha_s^2. The released
coefficient convention is

 sigma = Born delta(1-w)
       + alpha_s^3/(pi s v) [c_D0 D0+c_D1 D1+c_delta delta(1-w)] + regular.

Aversa's Eq. (22) factors Born*alpha_s/(2pi), so its normalized endpoint
coefficients correspond to 2{c_D0,c_D1,c_delta}/[v B(v)]. Equation (41)
uses d sigma/dv=pi v*sigma_Born, which is also checked. The paper prints
2 C_i in Eq. (43); the checker compares C_i. Its log(s/M^2), log(s/M_f^2),
log(s/mu^2) are -LF,-LFF,-LR in the released tables, and Tr=Nf/2.

Finite quark-scheme conversion
In Eq. (23), ignoring regular functions which cannot contribute endpoint
plus distributions or deltas,

 f_qq/CF = (1+z^2) D1(z) - (3/2) D0(z) - (9/2+pi^2/3) delta(1-z),
 d_qq/CF = (1+z^2) D1(z) - (3/2) D0(z) + (2pi^2/3-9/2) delta(1-z).

The incoming-quark convolution has z=w and endpoint slope k=1. The
fragmentation convolution has z=1-v+vw and slope k=v. In both cases the
endpoint density divided by k equals Born. A D1 pullback contributes
{j1/k log k, j1/k, j1/(2k) log^2 k} to {D0,D1,delta}; a D0 pullback gives
{j1/k,0,j1/k log k}. Since 1+z^2=2 at z=1, the summed normalized scheme
shift is

 CF {2 log(v)-3, 4, log^2(v)-(3/2)log(v)+pi^2/3-9}.

Subtract this finite quark PDF/FF shift from the MSbar result before
comparison with Eq. (43) at CQ=0. For example, at Nc=3 the normalized D1
changes from 50/3 to 34/3. Omitting this conversion is a tested nonzero
control, rather than a change to the released MSbar coefficient.

Scope and checks
The regular NLO density is not part of this benchmark. All three endpoint
identities are checked as rational identities in generic v,Nc,Nf and the
three independent scale logarithms. Log(v) and Log(1-v) are treated as
independent algebraic symbols; no numerical sampling or branch-changing
log rules are needed. The physical region is 0<v<1. Finite, scale-independent
perturbations of each actual endpoint are required to fail the same
comparison, illustrating information beyond pole and scale cancellation.

Execution
AversaEndpoints.wls needs Mathematica and Coefficients/ProtonProton.wl;
it does not load FeynCalc, FORM, Tracer, Kira, or SubTropica. Run from the
repository root with an output directory outside the source tree:

 FEYNFACET_SOURCE="$PWD" FEYNFACET_BENCHMARK_OUTPUT="../aversa-check" \
   math -noprompt -script Checks/AversaEndpoints.wls

The output directory receives endpoints.wl and summary.json. Keep generated
execution artifacts outside a source release. Existing coefficient tables
are read and hash-checked without modification.
